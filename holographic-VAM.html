Multi-beam holographic volumetric additive manufacturing

Tomographic printing builds a three-dimensional dose from many intensity-only projections. Holographic projection adds control over the phase of light, so that each beam can itself carry depth information. We developed an intensity-coupled phase-retrieval framework that jointly optimizes the phase masks of several holographic beams, so that their combined, nonlinearly thresholded dose reproduces a target volume.

The framework compares additive, subtractive, multiplicative and holo-tomographic beam configurations and examines the resolution limits that each imposes. Optimizing the beams jointly, rather than one at a time, reduces the reconstruction error in every configuration studied. The work extends the inverse-design view of volumetric printing from amplitude to phase, and connects to our broader effort to make volumetric printing faster and finer.
