Mechanics of nanoimprint: stamp design, resist flow and demolding

Nanoimprint lithography replicates nanoscale patterns by pressing a stamp into a thin polymer or resist film. Whether the pattern is faithful depends on contact mechanics: how the stamp deflects, how the resist flows beneath it, and how the interface separates at demolding. Residual-layer thickness, incomplete cavity filling and etch-depth variation are all consequences of these coupled solid and fluid mechanics, and they depend strongly on the layout of the pattern.

Building on the contact-mechanics tradition of K.L. Johnson, we developed simulation methods that represent the stamp through its elastic point-load response and the resist through thin-film flow, making full-chip and full-wafer prediction computationally practical. The same framework supports design: a computational method for two-layered elastic stamps (a Journal of Applied Physics Featured Article) shows how stamp architecture can equalize residual-layer thickness. A simple razor-blade test measures the energy needed to demold a stamp. The methods are protected by two US patents and underpin our nanoimprint simulation software.
