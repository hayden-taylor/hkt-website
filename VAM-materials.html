Materials for tomographic volumetric additive manufacturing: from hydrogels to glass

Because volumetric printing does not recoat or move layers, it can shape materials that are difficult for layer-based methods: soft hydrogels loaded with living cells, highly viscous resins, and dense nanoparticle suspensions that are later sintered into glass or ceramic. This is why computed axial lithography has been adopted by many bioprinting laboratories.

We contributed to a community-wide review, written by nineteen authors from the leading groups in the field, of the materials used in tomographic volumetric printing. Our own contributions span fused-silica glass from nanocomposite resins, multi-oxide glasses and ceramics, sedimentation-free organogel photoresists that hold particles in suspension during printing, and, with Ghent University, a tunable thiol–ene poly(ε-caprolactone) platform for biomedical implants and scaffolds.
