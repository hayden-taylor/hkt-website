Contact mechanics of manufacturable, micropatterned dry adhesives

Geckos and many insects cling to surfaces using dense arrays of fine fibrils, which conform to roughness and multiply van der Waals contacts. Synthetic “dry adhesives” copy this by patterning polymer films with micron-scale pillars. The most elaborate tip shapes (mushrooms, spatulae) adhere best but are hard to mass-produce, whereas straight, flat-tipped pillars can be made cheaply by molding.

With Richard O’Rorke and Terry Steele at Nanyang Technological University, we reviewed the analytical contact-mechanics models and experimental evidence for adhesion enhancement by surface patterns, focusing on manufacturable flat-tipped pillar arrays. The review identified where existing models, which typically assume elastic materials and independent pillars, disagree with measurements, and set out what a rational, manufacturing-aware design framework must capture, including interactions between closely spaced pillars and viscoelasticity. The same contact-mechanics tools underpin our computational framework for designing two-layered elastic stamps for nanoimprint and microcontact printing.
