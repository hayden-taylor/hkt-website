Superhydrophobic and liquid-repellent surfaces for more efficient air conditioning

When water condenses on an air-conditioner’s aluminum fins it usually forms a continuous film, which insulates the fin and chokes the airflow. If it instead condenses as droplets that roll off (dropwise condensation), air-side heat transfer can improve by an order of magnitude. Achieving that requires surfaces that are extremely water-repellent, survive condensation, and can be made cheaply on aluminum.

We developed a bottom-up process that grows nanoporous zinc oxide films with strongly re-entrant features directly on aluminum. The films reach static water contact angles up to 178° with hysteresis below 3°, repel low-surface-tension liquids, and sustain dropwise condensation in flowing humid air. A statistical model of wetting on surfaces with heterogeneous pore geometries explains how such textures resist penetration by different liquids. We also developed polyvinylidene fluoride microsphere coatings, and a triple-hierarchical, lotus-inspired surface (TriSS) combining micro-domes, wrinkles and nanohairs that is exceptionally robust to impinging water. The coating technology is protected by US Patent 11,326,060 and was commercialized by the spin-out Nelumbo, founded by Lance Brockway and Liam Berryman. (Listen to a podcast about Nelumbo.)
