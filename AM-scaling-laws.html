Fundamental scaling relationships in additive manufacturing

How fast can a printer build, given the smallest feature it must resolve? With Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and UC San Diego we compiled volumetric build rate against minimum feature size for a wide range of additive manufacturing processes. The data show a steep, systematic trade-off that follows from how each process delivers material and energy: point by point, line by line, layer by layer, or all at once.

The analysis identifies which physical constraints set the frontier, places volumetric printing on it, and discusses what the scaling implies for future manufacturing and biomanufacturing systems. The aim is to design processes whose throughput scales favorably, not just faster machines.
