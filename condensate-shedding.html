Hierarchical surfaces for stable dropwise condensation in tropical air conditioning

In hot, humid climates such as Singapore’s, a large share of air-conditioning energy goes into removing moisture rather than lowering temperature. Condensed water that sits on the cooling coil adds thermal resistance and pressure drop. Through the Berkeley Education Alliance for Research in Singapore (BEARS), we asked whether heat-exchanger surfaces could be designed to shed condensate as quickly as it forms, so that latent and sensible cooling can be handled more efficiently.

Soochan (Kyle) Chung and Kristyn Kadala developed hierarchically structured surfaces that combine photolithographically defined micro-domes or micro-pillars with a conformal nanoporous zinc oxide coating. Surfaces incorporating micro-domes sustained stable dropwise condensation, with droplets nucleating, coalescing and departing rather than flooding the texture. A scalable version of the process on aluminum points toward manufacturable, condensation-shedding heat exchangers.
