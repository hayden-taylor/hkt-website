From pixels to voxels: the physics and chemistry of volumetric 3D printing

Every volumetric printing method, whether tomographic, light-sheet or multiphoton, has to solve the same underlying problem: light passes through the whole build volume, yet material must solidify only where intended. That requires a nonlinearity somewhere in the process, supplied by oxygen inhibition, photochemical switching, two-step excitation, or a threshold in gelation.

With Sandra Van Vlierberghe’s group at Ghent University we wrote a mechanistic perspective that classifies volumetric printing methods by where their nonlinearity comes from, and relates the photochemistry to the achievable resolution, speed and material range. The article was highlighted in Progress in Polymer Science’s “Rising Stars in Polymer Science” series. In a 2024 Nature News & Views piece, Hayden Taylor discussed rapid printing at the curved surface of a liquid and its potential for in-space manufacturing.
